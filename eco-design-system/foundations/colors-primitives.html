Foundations

Colors

Color in ECO is semantic. A token says what a color is for, not what it looks like, so text, surfaces, borders and status each have their own family and a component never needs a raw hex value.

The base palette that the semantic tokens derive from, read live from the token package. Primitives are references: a component uses a semantic token, never a primitive.

About primitive colors

Primitive colors are our base palette, which the semantic palette derives from. Below are the colors that define it. They are the references the semantic tokens point to: a component uses the semantic token, never the primitive.

Every swatch shows which semantic tokens use it. If you need a color that no primitive has, consult a designer before adding one.

Neutral (grey)

The neutral palette is foundational and dominant in our UI, which makes it easier to add specific brand colors and system colors and make them stand out.

Neutral colors apply to most backgrounds, text and shapes. They do not typically have a meaning of their own, though they can imply things like disabled states.

A mainly grey palette gives the designs nuance and creates opportunities for a purposeful use of color, which makes a good user experience. Each screen should be dominated by the greys and the core colors of black, white and the appropriate brand color for accent.

System

The system colors help communicate messages. Use them for elements such as badges, alert and critical message boxes, or areas and sections that require immediate attention.

Concept brand colors

Alligo concept brands each have their own set of these colors. They put some flavor into the site and accent the brand that it represents.

Brand colors are mostly placed in common elements that occur throughout the site, which leaves the other colors, for example the customer price and the system colors, to give the visual aid they are there for.
