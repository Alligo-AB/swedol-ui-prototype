Overview
A section is a full-width block of a page. It divides the content into clear parts and gives each part the white space it needs, with a padding that steps at the five ECO breakpoints.
A section has two surfaces (white and grey), a first-section variant with less top padding, and a zeroed variant for five special cases. The same file also holds the page divider, a thin line between two sections, and the My Pages title block with its fixed spacing.
This documentation has three pages. Examples & Guidelines (this page) shows when and how to use a section. Specifications holds the values: tokens, padding per breakpoint, measurements and requirements. Code shows how to add one to a page.




Usage
When to use
Use a section for every distinct part of a page layout. Use it to:
  • Divide a page into clear parts, such as a hero, a product list and an FAQ
  • Give each part the white space that separates it from the next
  • Mark a clear change of content with a grey surface
  • Make the side margin follow the grid and the 1200px maximum width
When not to use
Keep the built-in padding
The padding is the white space that divides the content. Turn it off only in the five cases under Variants, and with a clear purpose. Everywhere else the content needs the air.
Do not stack the air
A page divider has its own 48px of padding. Between two sections that already have padding, zero the divider’s vertical padding, or the two amounts of air add up.
For a line inside content
A page divider is a boundary between two page sections. To separate items inside content, such as list rows, use the Divider component.

  • Use --px-page for the divider’s sides.
  • Stack a divider’s 48px on a section’s own padding.
  • Use zeroed padding without a clear purpose.




Anatomy
Section
  1. Padding, top. The space above the content. It steps at each breakpoint, and the first section has less.
  2. Padding, bottom. The space below the content. It steps at each breakpoint.
  3. Surface. The background: white (surface-raised-primary) or grey (surface-raised-secondary).
  4. Content container. The content area. The side margin comes from --px-full, so the content stays inside the grid.




Variants
A section varies in surface, position and padding. The page divider and the My Pages title block are part of the same file.
Surface
White is the default. A page should start with a white section or use white only. Grey marks a clear change of content.
Position
The first section on a page has less top padding, because the breadcrumb or the header sits right above it. Mark it with .section--first.

Sizes
A section has no sizes. Its padding changes with the breakpoint and with the position (first or other), at all five ECO breakpoints. The values are under Sizes in Specifications, with the row for your window width marked.




Layout & Appearance
Alignment
The content starts at the side margin --px-full and ends at the same margin on the other side. The margin follows the grid (16px xs, 32px sm and md, 40px lg) and centers the content against the 1200px maximum width on wide screens.
Placement
The first section on a page follows the breadcrumb directly and carries .section--first. Later sections follow each other with no gap: the padding of each is the white space between the parts. On My Pages, the page title block comes before the first section.
Width
A section is always as wide as the window. Only the content inside it is limited to 1200px.
Appearance
The default surface is white. By making white the base color of the page, a clear and calm structure is created, and a grey section stands out as a change. Exceptions exist, such as product list pages and My Pages. Other backgrounds need design approval.




Behavior
A section is not interactive. It has no hover, focus or disabled state and it does not animate.
Breakpoints
The padding steps at 640px, 769px, 1024px and 1281px, so a section has five sets of values. Unlike most components, sm (640 to 768px) has its own values. The side margin also follows the breakpoint.




Accessibility
All requirements are listed under Accessibility Requirements in Specifications.
Keyboard support
None. A section and a page divider are not focusable.
Screen reader support
Give each section a heading, so the parts of the page can be found. A <section> becomes a landmark only when it has an accessible name, for example with aria-labelledby that points to its heading. The page divider is an empty div and is not announced.
