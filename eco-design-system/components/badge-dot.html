Overview
A dot is a small colored circle, without text, that marks a status. Use it next to a text or an icon, or on the top right corner of an icon or a text, to show that a status has changed or that something needs attention.
A short summary lives here; the three pages are: Examples & Guidelines (this page), Specifications and Code.




Usage
When to use
  • Show that a status has changed, next to a label.
  • Draw attention to an icon or a text link: place the dot on its top right corner.
  • Give more weight to an existing status with a color.
When not to use
  • On its own. A dot never stands alone: it needs a label or an icon next to it.
  • For critical alerts. Dots are an indication, not an alert: use an inline notification.
  • Stacked, or close to other dots.
  • As the only carrier of meaning: color is not enough (WCAG 1.4.1).

Anatomy
  1. Dot. A circle without text. Its color is the status: Danger, Warning, Success, Information or Neutral. Large 10px, Medium 8px, Small 6px.
  2. Label or icon. Not part of the dot. A dot sits next to a text, or on the top right corner of an icon, and the label says what the status is.

Layout & Appearance
Placement
Use a dot next to a text, or on the top right corner of an icon or of a text. Keep the dot away from other dots.




Accessibility
A dot has no text. All requirements are listed under Accessibility Requirements in Specifications.
Screen reader support
A dot is decorative: aria-hidden="true". If the status matters, say it in text: next to the dot, in an aria-label on the icon button (“Messages, new”) or in visually hidden text.
Color is not the only carrier
Pair the dot with a label or an icon that says the same thing.
