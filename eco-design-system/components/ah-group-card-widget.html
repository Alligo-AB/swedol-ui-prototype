Article Highlight Group Card

Cards that promote articles, campaigns and brands, placed in a grid or in a carousel. A card can have an image, logos and graphics, badges, text, links and a button.

The anatomy of the card, the brand elements, the variants and when to use them.

Examples & GuidelinesStoryblok

Anatomy

A card is built from an image holder and a footer. Every part except the image is optional. Figma: Article Highlight Card Prototypes for Anatomy.

  • 1

    Image. The image, a color or a video, with an optional overlay, brand elements and logo.

  • 2

    Badge. A short label on the image, for example “Nyhet”.

  • 3

    Preheadline. Also called eyebrow. It gives context.

  • 4

    Title. The headline of the card.

  • 5

    Supporting text 1. Body text before the list.

  • 6

    Bulletlist. Bullet, numbered or checkmark list.

  • 7

    Supporting text 2. Body text after the list.

  • 8

    Links. Extra links, with or without icons, stacked or divided.

  • 9

    Date. A date or another short, related note.

  • 10

    Action link or button. The call to action of the card.

Brand elements

Brand elements sit on the image, never in the footer. They are part of the picture, so they follow the image when it is cropped. The two examples show a campaign concept and a discount with a brand logo.

  • 2

    Power Deal border. The graphic stripes at the top and bottom edge. Use it together with the logo.

Variants

A card can be used in many ways, depending on how the settings are combined. The grid shows Large cards (left) and Small cards (right) with the same content, in four setups with and without footer padding, border and badge. See 6. Card footer for the settings. Figma: AH Highlight Card Variants.

Usage

Guidance taken from the settings of the widget. See the Storyblok tab for each field.

Do

  • Use Footer Padding, Footer Border and Card Size in the same way on every card in a group or a concept.
  • Use Card Size Small in a layout with many cards.
  • Use the action link in the first place; keep the button for the one action that must stand out.
  • Keep a card that leads to an article or a campaign direct: a short title and little text.
  • Use Full Stroke Border when the image could blend into the page background.

Do not

  • Mix cards with and without Footer Padding in one group.
  • Use long lists in a card. Cards in a row get the same height, so one tall card makes the others tall.
  • Turn on Active Card and add extra links on the same card.
  • Put brand elements in the footer. They belong on the image.
