Banner

A banner carries a page-wide or system-level message: an outage, planned maintenance, a campaign or a notice to one customer group. It sits at the top and stays until it is closed or the situation changes.

Banners are set up by an administrator, not by user actions. See Placement and priority for where they go and which one wins. Specification: .claude/skills/eco-banner-notification/SKILL.md.

Variants

    
Three kinds of banner. Small is the everyday banner for status and campaigns. Large is a richer message for signed-in customers, shown on My Pages only. Promotion is for brand campaigns and has no status.

    
Breakpoint
      

    
Small

    
One line of text with an optional uppercase title and an optional inline link before the ×. Height 48px on desktop, 40px on mobile. The first two examples carry a link.

    

    
Large

    
A big icon, a heading, body copy and an action group with buttons and an optional link. Use it for a message to a signed-in customer or customer group, not for a one-line status.

    

    
Promotion

    
A centered campaign line in black, white or the brand’s own accent color. Strong and Weak read the accent tokens, so they are lime on Swedol and red on Tools without any extra code.

    

  






  

    
States

    
Each status in three emphasis levels. Strong is a solid color with white text and is the loudest, Weak is tinted, Weaker is white with a colored edge and a shadow. A banner has no hover or focus state of its own; only the × and its links do.

Placement and priority

    
An administrator sets up each banner: its text and size, which pages it shows on, which customers or industries (SNI codes) it targets, and an optional date range. Three of those decisions are shown below.

Every banner can also target one or more SNI codes (industry codes), one or more customer IDs, and a start and end date. Norway and Finland use the equivalent industry classification of their own country where one exists (for example SN2007 in Norway and TOL 2008 in Finland), in the same field as the Swedish SNI code.

    
Which banner wins

    
When more than one banner matches the same visitor and place, only the one with the highest priority shows. The administrator labels each banner with one of four priorities: 1 is strongest. On top of those sits level 0, Opt-out: a company can choose to see no banners at all, and that choice overrides every level, alerts included.

Usage

    
Use a banner for what concerns the whole page or system, not for feedback on one action.

    
The message…Use

    

      
Do
  • Use Strong only for what is urgent: outages, impersonation.
  • Use Weaker for general information and offers.
  • Use Large only on My Pages.
  • Match the icon to the status color.

      
Don’t
  • Show several banners in the same place. Priority picks one.
  • Use a banner for the result of a click. Use a Toast or Inline.
  • Hardcode a brand color in a Promotion banner. Use the accent tokens.
  • Use Large for a one-line message.
